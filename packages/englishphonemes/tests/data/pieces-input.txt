The old lighthouse stood at the edge of the cliff, and every evening the keeper climbed the long spiral staircase to light the great lamp. Ships far out at sea trusted that light; they steered by it through storms, through fog, and through the long dark nights of winter. Nobody in the village could remember a time when it had failed, and the children grew up believing that the lamp would burn forever. But one stormy night in November, the wind tore the shutters from the windows, and the sea rose higher than anyone had ever seen. The keeper, who was nearly seventy years old, held the lamp steady with both hands while the tower shook around him. At dawn the storm passed, the sea grew calm, and three small boats returned safely to the harbour; the fishermen said that without the light they would never have found their way home. The village gathered on the cliff that morning to thank the keeper, who only smiled, shrugged his shoulders, and asked whether anyone had brought breakfast.
