The Cone library

3D

Shapes, meshes, animation, physics, rendering, XR, worlds and interfaces for three-dimensional programs.

Shapes and sculpting

  • sdf [Windows and Linux]: Signed distance fields: shapes as functions from a point to its distance from the surface.
  • sdfmesh [Windows and Linux]: Any signed distance field sampled on a grid and made into a manifold mesh of quads, on the CPU or the GPU.
  • sculpt [Windows and Linux]: Edit-mode tools on polygon meshes: bevel, subdivision, multires and the like, rebuilt from Blender's manual.
  • sculptbrush [Windows and Linux]: Sculpt Mode's brushes and stroke engine on a fixed-topology mesh; a recipe of strokes replays to the same bits.
  • shapefit [Windows and Linux]: Deterministic instruments for deciding how to shape a mesh: fits, landmark alignment and measures.
  • skinmesh [Windows and Linux]: A quad mesh grown from a graph of nodes and edges, with a rig from the same graph.
  • quadremesh [Windows and Linux]: Automatic quad retopology: a mesh of triangles or polygons becomes a mesh of quads at a face count asked for.
  • morphogen [Windows and Linux]: Form that arises from growth rules (morphogenesis), made as graphs of points.
  • starship [Windows and Linux]: Elizabeth's "Starship 1", the skeletal dragon ship, reproduced.

Meshes and geometry

  • geomath [Windows and Linux]: The numbers 2-D and 3-D worlds are built from: vectors, matrices, quaternions, colours.
  • meshsolve [Windows and Linux]: Sparse linear solves on meshes: a sparse matrix, the cotangent Laplacian and a conjugate-gradient solver, shared by fairing and fitting.
  • mesh [Windows and Linux]: Surfaces made of polygons in the two forms the industry keeps them in: indexed triangles and a half-edge polygon mesh.
  • spatialindex [Windows and Linux]: Where things are, asked three ways: which boxes a ray, a box, a ball or a frustum meets.
  • autolod [Windows and Linux]: Levels of detail for any mesh, made automatically, ending in an octahedral impostor.
  • gltfimport [Windows and Linux]: glTF 2.0 models read into plain data: meshes, materials, a node tree, skins and animations.

Procedural nature and surfaces

  • noise [Windows and Linux]: Coherent noise for displacement and procedural textures, the same on the CPU and (mostly) bit for bit on the GPU.
  • noiserock [Windows and Linux]: Rocks as meshes, from a sphere cut by planes and roughened by noise.
  • weberpenn [Windows and Linux]: Trees grown by Weber and Penn's parametric method, as a skeleton of stems and then meshes.
  • leafrosette [Windows and Linux]: Low plants that grow as a ring of blades from one point, as meshes.
  • groundcover [Windows and Linux]: Lush, lived-in ground from rules.
  • terraingen [Windows and Linux]: Landscapes from parameters: a terrain described as data, then eroded and made.
  • terrainlod [Windows and Linux]: Ground that refines itself with distance, over any height function or heightmap tiles.
  • oceanwaves [Windows and Linux]: An FFT ocean, calm to storm, that can be stepped, queried and drawn.
  • analyticdaylight [Windows and Linux]: The light of a clear day and its night as functions of the time of day: sun, moon, sky colour.
  • boidflock [Windows and Linux]: Flocks of small birds flying round a home point over rising and falling ground, and how each flaps.
  • surfacepatterns [Windows and Linux]: Procedural patterns that fill a tile with a recognisable surface (boards, shingles, stone, bark, brick) and its material maps.
  • pbrmaterial [Windows and Linux]: What goes into a physically based material: its parameters, and textures of them baked from procedural noise.

Animation and rigs

  • bonepose [Windows and Linux]: A skeleton as data, a pose of it, forward kinematics and two-bone inverse kinematics.
  • poseik [Windows and Linux]: Inverse kinematics, foot placement, look-at, hand reach, pose blending and a small animation state machine.
  • animlayers [Windows and Linux]: Layered animation on top of a base pose.
  • keyframetracks [Windows and Linux]: Property animation: keyframes with easing, grouped into clips, played with cross-fades, layers and events.
  • tweenstack [Windows and Linux]: Animators that run over time and write the value of a field each tick.
  • creaturerig [Windows and Linux]: Any creature's skeleton from data, and the weights that bind a mesh to it.
  • facerig [Windows and Linux]: A face that is alive, for any face mesh described by data.
  • handpose [Windows and Linux]: Five fingers that can hold, turn, press and knead: grip shapes and hands on handles.
  • humanbody [Windows and Linux]: People of game quality as data: measures, a look and an outfit, made into a skinned body.
  • livingbody [Windows and Linux]: Bodies that look alive: breathing and the other small continual motions of a standing person.
  • walkgait [Windows and Linux]: A phase-driven biped walk with feet planted on the ground, an idle layer and a hard-landing crouch.
  • leggedgait [Windows and Linux]: Gaits as data: a footfall pattern for N legs, and what goes with it.
  • wingflight [Windows and Linux]: Wings and flight for any rig with wing chains.
  • strandflow [Windows and Linux]: Strands in a flow: hair, manes, tassels and tentacles moving in air or water, drawn as cards or tubes.
  • splinetrack [Windows and Linux]: Anything that runs on a track: splines, junctions and switches, and what moves along them.

Physics

  • rigidcollide [Windows and Linux]: The collision half of a Jolt-like rigid-body engine: shapes, broad phase and contacts.
  • rigiddynamics [Windows and Linux]: The solver half of a Jolt-like rigid-body engine: a World of bodies, joints and queries stepped at a fixed rate.
  • ragdollrig [Windows and Linux]: Ragdolls for bonepose's skeletons over rigiddynamics, limp or powered towards a pose.
  • xpbdsoftbody [Windows and Linux]: Cloth, ropes, chains and inflated envelopes by extended position-based dynamics.
  • clothrope [Windows and Linux]: Cloth and rope that push and pull on the world, joining xpbdsoftbody and rigiddynamics.
  • garmentdrape [Windows and Linux]: Garments draped on a body, with cloth materials: weave, sheen, wear and dirt.
  • buoyancy [Windows and Linux]: Bodies that float, and a boat from data: hull, engine, rudder.
  • wheelvehicle [Windows and Linux]: Anything on wheels, from data: engine, gearbox, differential, suspension.

Rendering and the GPU

  • vulkan [Windows and Linux]: Raw bindings to Vulkan 1.3, written by hand from the specification.
  • gpu [Windows and Linux]: Cone's own thin GPU layer, shaped like WebGPU's objects, with Vulkan underneath.
  • gpuwork [Windows and Linux]: Handing wide, repetitive work to the GPU: kernels run per invocation, data moved only when it must.
  • render [Windows and Linux]: Drawing lit 3-D meshes on the GPU: cameras, lights, materials and image-quality passes.
  • vfx [Windows and Linux]: Visual effects drawn by the GPU, beginning with stateless particle emitters.
  • effectsfx [Windows and Linux]: Effects at the standard of the best shooters, defined as data and drawn by the GPU.
  • msdftext [Windows and Linux]: Text for the world, sharp at every size and angle, from TrueType fonts as an MSDF atlas.
  • vector2d [Windows and Linux]: 2-D vector graphics drawn on the GPU, anti-aliased, with text.
  • diagramlayout [Windows and Linux]: Diagrams from data, laid out and routed, drawn in 2-D by vector2d and in 3-D by render.

XR

  • openxr [Windows and Linux]: Raw bindings to OpenXR 1.0 and 1.1, the part a headset's Vulkan swapchain needs.

Worlds, input and interfaces

  • window [Windows and Linux]: A window to render into, opened through SDL, and the glue of a render loop around it.
  • sdl [Windows and Linux]: Raw bindings to SDL3: video, windows, Vulkan surfaces, clocks, the event queue and audio streams.
  • input [Windows and Linux]: The keyboard and the mouse as a program reads them: raw events, state, and named actions with bindings.
  • controls [Windows and Linux]: Cameras steered by input actions, giving back a pose.
  • frame [Windows and Linux]: The loop a world runs in: the owner calls the world, and the world never owns a 'while running'.
  • worldinteract [Windows and Linux]: Action scenarios for a world as pure logic: doors, targeting, held things, properties, events and triggers.
  • navmesh [Windows and Linux]: Finding the way: a navigation mesh, A* over it, funnel paths, raycasts, off-mesh links and steering.
  • npcbehave [Windows and Linux]: What a non-player character decides and how it moves: behaviour trees, utility, state machines and steering.
  • editgizmo [Windows and Linux]: Editing 3-D in place: pick by ray, move/rotate/scale handles, snapping, selection outlines, undo and redo.
  • uilayout [Windows and Linux]: The layout and interaction core under widgets, for screen overlays and panels placed in a 3-D world.
  • uipickers [Windows and Linux]: The controls an editor needs and uilayout lacks, and a property inspector built from a description of fields.
